About

A little more about my background, research path, and the kinds of technical questions I like working on.

Background

I am an electrical engineering student at the Colorado School of Mines, graduating in May 2027. My work has included machine learning, signal processing, biomechanics, spectroscopy, robotics, embedded systems, heliophysics, and exoplanet research.

Current work

I am currently a Research Assistant at NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory. My work includes a 1D convolutional neural network for detecting anomalous exoplanet transit signals in noisy light-curve data, along with software that identifies and categorizes solar-flare detection errors within the Excalibur pipeline.

Recent international research

From May through August 2026, I conducted research at ETH Zürich's Laboratory for Movement Biomechanics. I developed a MATLAB-based modeling pipeline using wearable IMU data to estimate foot placement and evaluate gait stability.

Earlier NASA research

At NASA Goddard Space Flight Center, I processed H-Lyman-alpha intensity data in JupyterHub and MATLAB and used a 28-layer residual neural network to model emission patterns for exospheric mapping research.

What connects the projects

I am most interested in research where engineering tools can make complex scientific data easier to understand, validate, and use. I especially enjoy projects that sit between physical systems and data-driven modeling.
